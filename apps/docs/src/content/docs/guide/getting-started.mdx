Someone set up Doota and created an account for you. This walks you through your
first few minutes — no technical know-how needed.

## 1. Find your invite

Check the inbox of your **personal email** (the one you gave the person who set
you up — a Gmail or similar). You'll have a message from Doota with three things:

- Your new **Doota email address** (something like `you@yourcompany.com`).
- A **temporary password**.
- A link to **confirm this recovery email**.

<Aside type="tip" title="Can't find it?">
  Check spam. If it's still missing, ask whoever set up Doota to resend the
  invite.
</Aside>

## 2. Sign in

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Doota">
    Go to the Doota web address you were given (for example
    `mail.yourcompany.com`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your details">
    Type your new **Doota email** and the **temporary password** from the invite.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Continue">
    Doota signs you in and walks you through a short setup.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 3. Finish setup

The first time you sign in, Doota asks you to do a few quick things. It won't let
you into your mail until they're done — they keep your account yours.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose your own password">
    Replace the temporary password with one only you know. Pick something long
    and hard to guess.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your recovery email">
    This is a personal address (not your Doota one) used to get you back in if
    you're ever locked out. Click the confirm link from the invite, or ask Doota
    to resend it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a second layer (if asked)">
    Depending on your role, Doota may ask you to set up a **passkey** or an
    **authenticator app**. See [Signing in safely](/guide/account-security) —
    it's quick and makes your account much harder to break into.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Save your backup codes">
  If you set up an authenticator app, Doota shows a set of **backup codes**.
  Save them somewhere safe (a password manager is ideal). They're how you get in
  if you lose your phone.
</Aside>

## 4. Send your first message

You're in. Your mail looks like a chat:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a message">
    Click **compose** (usually a pencil or "new" button).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill it in">
    Add who it's going to, a subject, and your message — just like any email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send">
    Hit send. You've got a few seconds to **undo** if you spot a typo. Watch it
    move from _sending_ to _delivered_ right in the conversation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That's it — you're up and running.
