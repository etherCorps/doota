Your Doota address is also how you sign in — and you can't read your mail until
you're in. So Doota gives you simple, strong ways to sign in, and a reliable way
back if you're ever locked out. You'll find all of this in your **account
settings**, under security.

## Ways to sign in

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Password + code">
    Enter your Doota email and password. If you've turned on extra protection,
    Doota also asks for a **6-digit code** from an authenticator app on your
    phone. That's it — you're in.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Passkey">
    A passkey lets you sign in with your **fingerprint, face, or device PIN** —
    no password to remember, and nothing to type. It's the easiest _and_ safest
    option. Set one up once, then it's a tap from then on.
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Adding extra protection

An authenticator app (like Google Authenticator, 1Password, or Authy) gives you
a rotating 6-digit code — a second lock on your account so a stolen password
isn't enough on its own. Doota walks you through setting it up.

<Aside type="caution" title="Save your backup codes">
  When you set up an authenticator, Doota shows a list of **backup codes**. Save
  them somewhere safe. If you ever lose your phone, a backup code is how you get
  back in.
</Aside>

## Passkeys

A passkey is the modern, phishing-proof way to sign in — your device proves it's
you. You can add more than one (laptop, phone, a hardware key) so you're never
stuck. Add and manage them in your security settings.

## Your recovery email

Because you can't read your Doota mail until you're signed in, Doota sends
account help to a **different, personal address** (like your own Gmail). Set and
confirm it in security settings.

If you ever forget your password, ask Doota to reset it — the reset link goes to
that personal recovery address, never to your Doota one.

## Changing your password

- **From the sign-in screen** (locked out): use "forgot password" and Doota
  emails a reset link to your recovery address.
- **While signed in**: change it in security settings. For safety, Doota asks
  for both a one-time code and your current password before the change takes
  effect.

<Aside type="tip" title="Locked out completely?">
  Your **backup codes** are the first way back in. If none of these work, the
  person who runs your Doota can reset your account for you.
</Aside>
